More Rosé Pine, measured legible

Three audit agents went over the real CSS: WCAG contrast math on every token pairing, a census of where each accent actually paints, and the vibecoded-design-tells checklist. Everything below is their evidence, acted on, then re-measured. Click any element to annotate it; the form at the bottom queues the open decisions.

The verdict, in three lines

① The palette was never the problem — Rosé Pine Moon is your chosen reference and stays. The problem was four of its six accents painting almost nothing (rose and pine: zero pixels) while iris wore everything.

② The “hard to read” complaint had a measurable shape: every failure was an accent ink sitting on a wash of its own hue — worst case 1.24:1, an overdue marker that effectively did not exist — plus 112 style rules under 10px.

③ The tells audit found real bugs, not just style: dead CSS from retired pages was silently squeezing the live month grid to 44px tall and restyling the desk rows.

Hue = meaning — the deliberate assignment

Written into the token block so every future use can say why. This is the anti-monotone system: six accents, six jobs, never decoration.

iristhe assistant & the brand — anything machine-authored
foamthe client's side & live evidence — their bubbles, their numbers
pinestructure & steady state — fills and dots only, never text
goldtime & money — deadlines, maturities, the calls badge
rosethe relationship — personal facts worth remembering
lovealarm, and only alarm

Contrast receipts — every failing pair, before → after

Measured with WCAG 2.1 math, not eyeballed. 4.5:1 is the pass line for normal text. The repair pattern: fills mix toward the background (denser, not brighter), inks step up to their lighter -hi tier, and the two hottest chip states flip to solid accent with dark ink.
